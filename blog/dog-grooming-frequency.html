How Often Should You Groom Your Dog?

A practical guide to grooming frequency for dogs in El Paso, including coat, lifestyle, and comfort factors.

Petssible journal

There is no single grooming schedule that fits every dog. Coat type, activity level, shedding, and home care all influence how often your dog may need a professional visit. A short-haired dog may only need occasional bathing and nail care, while a curly or long-coated dog can benefit from regular brushing and trims.

Start with your dog’s coat and routine

Dogs with coats that mat easily need attention before tangles become uncomfortable. Regular brushing at home helps, and a grooming team can talk through a sensible schedule based on what they see in your dog’s coat. Active dogs may also need baths more often than dogs who stay mostly indoors.

Do not overlook nails and skin

Nail trims are part of comfort, not just appearance. If you hear nails clicking on the floor, it may be time to ask about a trim. If your dog has skin sensitivity, coat changes, or a grooming concern, share it before the visit so the team can plan with care.
